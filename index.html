Research projects

Neural synchrony in cocaine users (SUDMEX dataset)

Undergraduate research · Nanyang Technological University
Context
Substance use disorder research benefits from open neuroimaging data. I designed a project on how brain regions synchronise in people who use cocaine (N = 138).
Method
Analysis in R on the SUDMEX dataset; literature synthesis across 50 sources; reproducible data pipelines built with Claude AI assistance.
Findings
Characterised inter-regional synchrony patterns in the cohort, presented as a data-driven talk.
Impact
Won Best Datablitz Presentation at the AS4SAN neuroscience conference.

Using AI well in research

I treat AI as a research collaborator that still needs supervision. I use Claude and Gemini to accelerate literature synthesis, data cleaning and scripting, then verify every output against the source data and the analysis plan.

  • Prompt engineering: structured, constraint-driven prompts for literature mapping, code review and analysis checks.
  • Automated workflows: scripted pipelines for survey processing and longitudinal analysis in R, plus personal automations such as an email-based opportunity digest linked to my calendar.
  • Web and tool building: I design and build my own sites and small tools, including this one.
  • Responsible use: I follow data governance and privacy protocols, keeping sensitive participant data out of AI tools unless it is properly protected.
